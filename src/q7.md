> **Prompt.** A stock pays \$2.50 in years 1–3, \$3.50 in years 4–6, then \$5.00 **forever** from year 7 onward. Required return: 8%. Price today?

## Intuition — three blocks, glued together

Whenever dividends change shape, **split the stream into clean blocks**:

- **Block A.** 3 years of \$2.50 — simple annuity starting next year.
- **Block B.** 3 years of \$3.50 starting in year 4 — annuity, but delayed 3 years.
- **Block C.** \$5.00 perpetuity starting in year 7 — value the perpetuity **at year 6** (one period before the first cash flow), then discount that single number back six years.

The year-6 timing on Block C is what students miss. The perpetuity formula <em>D/r</em> gives the value **one period before** the first cash flow. First flow is year 7, so <em>D/r</em> values it at year 6. Divide by (1+r)<sup>6</sup>, not (1+r)<sup>7</sup>.

## The three blocks

```js
const pvA = pvStream([d1, d1, d1], r, 1);
const pvB = pvStream([d2, d2, d2], r, 4);
const perpAt6 = d3 / r;
const pvC = perpAt6 / Math.pow(1 + r, 6);
const price = pvA + pvB + pvC;
```
