> **Prompt.** Borrow \$5,000 today. Year 1 rate is 2.5%, year 2 rate is 8%. What lump sum pays the loan off at the end of year 2?

## Intuition

When the rate **changes between periods** you cannot use a single $(1+r)^n$ — there is no single <em>r</em>. You compound year by year. The \$5,000 grows by 2.5% in year 1 to a new balance, then *that* balance grows by 8% in year 2.

The common trap: **adding the rates**. People see "2.5% and 8%" and write \$5,000 × 1.105 = \$5,253. Wrong. Rates compound multiplicatively, never additively.
